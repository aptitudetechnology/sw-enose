# **Olfactory-Modeling-Data-and-Evidence**

# **Evidence Base and Feasibility of a Biologically Grounded Software Nose**

## **1\. Direct Answer**

Only a narrow prototype software nose can be built from real receptor data today, not a general biological nose. It is **established** that M2OR compiles 51,395 olfactory receptor (OR)–odorant pairs across 1,246 ORs and 768 molecules (PMC10767820)1, competitive binding predicts responses within 15% for up to 12 odorants overall and reaches median RMSE below 0.1 for 12 of 15 human and mouse ORs on 21 binary/ternary mixtures in heterologous luciferase assays (PMC6511041)2, and *Drosophila* antennal-lobe divisive normalization has exponent 1.5 (PMC2866644)3, while modeling (10.1126/science.aam9868) formalizes cited mushroom-body expansion (50 projection neurons to 2,000 Kenyon cells, \~6 inputs each, top 5% active)4. It is **plausible** that extended competitive-binding models capture synergy and inhibition2. **Untested** in verified records are full-repertoire OR kinetics, held-out unseen-receptor accuracy, mammalian circuit parameters, and gas-sensing validation. The **strongest argument against** combines data sparsity, cell-assay artifacts, human pseudogenization, and cross-species circuit mismatch.

Two empirical limitations constrain how M2OR can parameterize a virtual nose. First, the **fraction of human ORs with at least one known agonist** is **not found in verified full-text records** (PMC10767820 does not report this figure)1. Only unverified truncated excerpts point to low single-screen deorphanization rates (PMC2733323 cites Saito et al. 2009 as finding *"only 23% of the tested mouse ORs and 4% of the human ORs showed responses to at least one of the 93 test odorants"*12, while PMC12770814 analyzes 385 human ORs and 510 odorants from M2OR without stating the active receptor fraction13, and PMC4003190 and PMC4412152 truncate before completing the deorphanization figures14). Second, M2OR's combined 11-species interaction matrix covers only 51,395 / (1,246 × 768\) \= 5.4% of possible receptor–molecule combinations, and many records provide only single-concentration binary calls rather than full concentration–response curves: *"In the majority of OR assays, compounds are typically evaluated individually against each receptor using transient mammalian cell line-based luciferase assays"*1, *"An experiment is labelled ‘responsive’ if the measured signal is considered as statistically different from the baseline by the authors. Otherwise it is labelled ‘non-responsive’"*, and *"Contrary to previous databases, M2OR includes either screening concentration or EC\_50\_ for all gathered experimental data."*1 M2OR also highlights persistent biological bottlenecks: *"Exploring the recognition spectrum of ORs have required the development of several cell biology protocols to overcome the challenge associated with expressing ORs on the surface of heterologous systems cells ()."*1

## **5\. Five Biological Processing Stages and Mixture Coding**

Verified full-text evidence covers **Stage 1** (Singh et al., 2019; primary experiments in cultured heterologous cells plus biophysical modeling)2, **Stage 4** (Olsen, Bhandawat & Wilson, 2010; primary *Drosophila* antennal-lobe electrophysiology)3, and the cited *Drosophila* circuit anatomy used in **Stage 5** (Dasgupta, Stevens & Navlakha, 2017; computational LSH study)4. Direct primary full-text sources for **Stage 2** convergence ratios, **Stage 3** adaptation time constants, and **mammalian piriform cortex (Stage 5\)** were not found in verified form in these searches.

The authors report: *"Dose–response curves were measured for 15 receptors (e.g., Fig. 1A) by stimulating the receptors across the full range of concentrations allowed by our assay (\[0,0.3 mM\]...)"*2, *"These 15 receptors were then stimulated with 21 mixtures (12 binary, 9 ternary) of eugenol, coumarin, and acetophenone..."*2, *"This simple framework predicts receptor responses to mixtures of up to 12 monomolecular odorants to within 15% of experimental observations"*2, and *"For most receptors (12 of 15), the root-mean-square error (rmse) ... was low (median below 0.1) and small relative to the observed response (median of rmse/observed response \= 0.16) and compared with the experimental SDs (median rmse/SD \= 1.2)."*2 Competitive binding inherently produces mixture suppression when a binary mixture's response lies between the two individual responses; however, 3 of 15 receptors showed larger discrepancies, mouse Olfr1104 required an explicit eugenol suppression term, and true synergy (exceeding single-component sums) or below-baseline inhibition require extensions such as facilitation, noncompetitive inhibition, multiple binding sites, or heterodimerization2. Unverified excerpts from PMC1271670 (screening eugenol antagonists at 100 µM in HEK293 cells)30 and PMID: 32620767 / PMC7335155 (reporting *"widespread antagonistic interactions in binary odor mixtures"* in glomerular and epithelial imaging of freely breathing mice)31 point toward in vivo relevance, though neither could be verified in full text here.

**Dasgupta, Stevens & Navlakha (2017)** (*Science* 358(6364):793–796, DOI 10.1126/science.aam9868, PMID 29123069, full text read)4 is a **computational modeling study**, not a primary experimental paper. Using cited *Drosophila* anatomy—*"There are 50 ORN types"*, *"Fifty PNs project to 2000 Kenyon cells (KCs), connected by a sparse, binary random connection matrix (9)"*, *"Each KC receives and sums the firing rates from about six randomly selected PNs (9)"*, and *"all but the highest-firing 5% of KCs are silenced"* by the APL neuron4—the authors prove that the circuit implements a locality-sensitive hashing (LSH) family. On machine-learning nearest-neighbor benchmarks (MAP across 50 trials, top 2% neighbors), the fly algorithm achieved a 20-fold computational savings over dense Gaussian LSH and outperformed standard LSH at equal operations (e.g., 44.8% vs. 16.0% on MNIST at hash length k \= 4\)4. Meanwhile, primary experimental studies of mammalian **piriform cortex** (Poo & Isaacson 2009; Stettler & Axel 2009\) appeared only as truncated abstracts or citations in unverified leads (PMC2896888, PMID: 19778513\) without verified active-cell percentages33.

## **7\. The Case Against**

The strongest empirical case against building a general-purpose software nose from existing biological receptor data rests first on extreme data sparsity and missing kinetic parameters. Even M2OR (PMC10767820, PMID 37870437), the largest curated multi-species dataset retrieved, covers only 51,395 unique pairs across 1,246 receptors and 768 odorants (5.4% of that 11-species matrix)1. Most human (396 intact genes), mouse (\~1,000 genes), and dog ORs lack experimental response profiles across chemical space, and many M2OR records supply only single-concentration binary (responsive/non-responsive) tags rather than the EC50 and efficacy (e\_i) parameters required by Stage 1 competitive-binding equations1.  
Second, almost all large-scale mammalian OR data reflect heterologous cell-culture artifacts rather than physiological olfactory sensory neurons. As M2OR notes, most screens rely on transient mammalian cell-line luciferase reporters that require specialized cell-biology protocols, exogenous G-proteins, co-transfected chaperones, and N-terminal tags just to traffic ORs to the cell surface1. Likewise, Singh et al. (PMC6511041, PMID 31000595\) measured steady-state luminescence in cultured heterologous cells capped at 0–0.3 mM2, omitting millisecond spike dynamics and intact tissue environment.  
Third, human receptor biology itself undermines any fixed, sequence-derived human sensor array. Niimura (2012) (PMC3308321, PMID 23024602\) shows that 52% of the human OR gene family consists of pseudogenes, \~35% of intact human OR coding regions are predicted inactive (compared with \~5% in mice), and \~15% and \~20% of functional human OR genes vary across individuals through copy-number variants and segregating pseudogenes5. Sequence databases alone therefore cannot specify which receptors are functional in an average human nose.  
Fourth, neither mixture behavior nor in silico deorphanization is solved at scale. While competitive binding predicts responses within 15% for up to 12 odorants overall and achieves median RMSE below 0.1 for 12 of 15 receptors tested on binary and ternary mixtures, 3 of 15 receptors (20%) deviated substantially, with mouse Olfr1104 requiring explicit eugenol suppression and synergy or below-baseline inhibition requiring additional non-competitive or multi-site mechanisms (PMC6511041)2. For unmeasured receptors, no retrieved study provided verified held-out accuracy on unseen ORs or odorants (UNVERIFIED leads only).  
Finally, combining mammalian receptor parameters with *Drosophila* circuit constants introduces an unresolved cross-species mismatch. Verified numerical constants for Stage 4 divisive normalization (Olsen et al., 2010, exponent 1.5, 500 ms spike window)3 and Stage 5 sparse expansion (Dasgupta et al., 2017, 50 PNs to 2,000 KCs, \~6 inputs/KC, top 5% active)4 come from *Drosophila*, whose receptors are homotetrameric ion channels rather than vertebrate GPCRs (\~396–\>1,000 ORs), and even *Drosophila* exhibits glomerulus-specific GABAergic gain (DM1 vs. VM7)3. Without verified mammalian convergence ratios, peripheral adaptation time constants (Stage 3), olfactory-bulb normalization constants, or piriform sparsity measurements, Stages 2 through 5 in any mammalian software nose remain largely ungrounded.
