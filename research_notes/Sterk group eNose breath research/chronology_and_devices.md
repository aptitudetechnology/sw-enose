# Sterk group (AMC / Amsterdam UMC) eNose breath research: chronology and device lineage

Method note: bibliographic data (title, first author, year, journal, PMID, DOI) were copied from PubMed E-utilities records (esummary/efetch) pulled on 2026-11-16, not from memory. "Abstract" means only the PubMed abstract was read. "Full text" means the Europe PMC/PMC full-text XML was retrieved and searched for the stated passages. It was not read cover to cover. A PubMed search `Sterk PJ[Author] AND (electronic nose OR eNose OR e-nose OR breathomics OR SpiroNose OR volatile organic compounds)` returned 74 records. A second search for SpiroNose/Breathomix/de Vries R eNose papers without Sterk returned 66 records, many of them off-topic. Only the key ones are listed below.

## Q1. Key publications, first to most recent

### Takeaway
The group went through four phases. (1) 2007-2013: small proof-of-concept case-control studies with the commercial Cyranose 320. Samples were collected in Tedlar bags, and analysis was PCA followed by linear/canonical discriminant analysis. Studies moved on to external validation by 2011. (2) 2013-2019: the U-BIOPRED severe-asthma work, using a composite "assembly" of four different eNoses plus GC-MS, with an emphasis on multi-site methodology. (3) 2015 onward: the in-house SpiroNose, a metal-oxide array at the back end of a spirometer pneumotachograph with ambient-air reference sensors. It moved the group from bags to point-of-care measurement. (4) 2019-2026: large SpiroNose cohorts (BreathCloud) and external or multicentre validation, mostly led by collaborators: anti-PD-1 response in lung cancer, early lung cancer in COPD, ILD, cystic fibrosis and post-COVID.

### Cited Findings (chronological; [A] = abstract read, [FT] = full text searched)

**Phase 1: Cyranose 320 era**
- 2007. Dragonieri S et al., "An electronic nose in the discrimination of patients with asthma and controls." J Allergy Clin Immunol 120(4):856-62. PMID 17658592, DOI 10.1016/j.jaci.2007.05.043 [A]. 40 subjects (10 mild asthma, 10 young controls, 10 severe asthma, 10 older controls). Breath was taken after 5 min tidal breathing of VOC-filtered air, one expiratory vital capacity was collected into a Tedlar bag, and the bag was sampled by **Cyranose 320** within 10 min. Analysis was LDA on principal-component reduction with cross-validation values (CVV). Results: mild asthma vs young controls CVV 100%, severe asthma vs older controls 90%, mild vs severe asthma only 65%. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17658592/
- 2009. Dragonieri S et al., "An electronic nose in the discrimination of patients with non-small cell lung cancer and COPD." Lung Cancer 64(2):166-70. PMID 18834643, DOI 10.1016/j.lungcan.2008.08.008 [A]. 30 subjects (10 NSCLC, 10 COPD, 10 controls). Cyranose 320 with Tedlar bags, VOC-filtered room air as baseline, analysed by the device's onboard statistical software. NSCLC vs COPD CVV 85%. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18834643/
- 2009. Fens N et al., "Exhaled breath profiling enables discrimination of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and asthma." Am J Respir Crit Care Med 180(11):1076-82. PMID 19713445, DOI 10.1164/rccm.200906-0939OC [A]. 90 subjects: asthma vs COPD accuracy 96%, COPD vs smoking controls only 66%. Methodological point: repeatability and reproducibility were assessed by measuring samples in duplicate on **two devices**. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19713445/
- 2010. Lazar Z et al., "Electronic nose breathprints are independent of acute changes in airway caliber in asthma." Sensors (Basel) 10(10):9127-38. PMID 22163399, DOI 10.3390/s101009127 [A]. 10 patients, Cyranose 320. Confounder study: methacholine-induced bronchoconstriction did not differ from the saline sham, but nebulised aerosol itself changed the breathprint. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22163399/
- 2011. Fens N et al., "External validation of exhaled breath profiling using an electronic nose in the discrimination of asthma with fixed airways obstruction and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease." Clin Exp Allergy 41(10):1371-8. PMID 21732998, DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2222.2011.03800.x [A]. 100 newly recruited patients were tested against a previous, independent training set, using PCA plus canonical discriminant analysis. Fixed asthma vs COPD accuracy 88% (AUC 0.95). This is an early **external validation** step. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21732998/
- Other Cyranose-era applications (titles only, from PubMed summaries): Fens 2010 pulmonary embolism, J Thromb Haemost, PMID 20860678; Fens 2011 COPD inflammatory subtype, Eur Respir J, PMID 21700610; Dragonieri 2012 malignant pleural mesothelioma, Lung Cancer, PMID 21924516; Dragonieri 2013 sarcoidosis, Respir Med, PMID 23647864; van der Schee 2013 sorbent-tube transport/storage, J Breath Res, PMID 23257711; Paff 2013 cystic fibrosis/PCD, J Cyst Fibros, PMID 23361110; de Heer 2013 invasive aspergillosis, J Clin Microbiol, PMID 23467602; Bos 2014 ventilator-associated pneumonia, Intensive Care Med, PMID 24615562; van der Schee 2015 rhinovirus wheeze in children, Eur Respir J, PMID 25323245. **Phase 2: U-BIOPRED (composite eNose platform plus GC-MS)**
- 2019 (online Dec 2018). Brinkman P et al. (U-BIOPRED Study Group; co-authors include D'Amico A, Pennazza G, Santonico M from the Tor Vergata/Rome groups and Vink TJ, Weda H from Philips-affiliated sites), "Identification and prospective stability of electronic nose (eNose)-derived inflammatory phenotypes in patients with severe asthma." J Allergy Clin Immunol 143(5):1811-1820.e7. PMID 30529449, DOI 10.1016/j.jaci.2018.10.058 [A]. 78 adults with severe asthma. Samples were analysed centrally by an "**assembly of eNoses**". Unsupervised Ward clustering with similarity-profile analysis and K-means gave 3 clusters, which were internally validated with partitioning around medoids and **topological data analysis**. Clusters were re-checked at 12-18 months. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30529449/
- The four devices in that assembly were Tor Vergata, Cyranose 320, Owlstone Lonestar and (by the same group's design) Common Invent. This comes from a secondary review: Licht JC & Grasemann H 2020, Int J Mol Sci, PMC7763696, which says "A four-eNose panel was used, including the Tor Vergata, Cyranose 320, Owlstone Lonest[ar]..." (the snippet was truncated) [FT]. — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7763696
- The same four-device assembly was confirmed from primary full text in a Sterk/Brinkman-co-authored mesothelioma paper. Lamote K et al. 2017, Oncotarget, PMID 29207669, PMC5710949 [FT]: "samples were analyzed by an assembly of four different eNoses, based on deviant sensor technologies: Cyranose C320, Tor Vergata eNose, Common Invent eNose, and Owlstone Lonestar ... The final eNose-based breath profiles were established by merging the sensor defections of all four devices." Samples were thermally desorbed from Tenax GR sorbent tubes. — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC5710949
- Sensor technologies of those devices, as described in the Licht & Grasemann review [FT]: Cyranose 320 is a carbon black-polymer array. Tor Vergata uses QCM sensors coated with metalloporphyrins. Common Invent uses MOS sensors. Owlstone Lonestar uses FAIMS (field asymmetric ion mobility spectrometry). SpiroNose uses cross-reactive MOS sensors. — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7763696
- 2018. Ahmed WM et al. (Sterk PJ, Fowler SJ senior), "Methodological considerations for large-scale breath analysis studies: lessons from the U-BIOPRED severe asthma project." J Breath Res 13(1):016001. PMID 30272570, DOI 10.1088/1752-7163/aae557 [A]. Covers batch variation and correction, between-site variation, storage and transport, and inter-instrument differences, and gives recommended best practices. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30272570/
- 2020 (online 2019). Brinkman P et al., "Exhaled volatile organic compounds as markers for medication use in asthma." Eur Respir J 55(2):1900544. PMID 31515400, DOI 10.1183/13993003.00544-2019 [A]. Used GC-tof-MS, not an eNose. 78 patients. Breath VOCs were linked to urinary salbutamol (AUC 82.1%) and oral corticosteroids (78.8%), with replication and independent validation. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31515400/

**Phase 3: SpiroNose development and validation**
- 2015. de Vries R, Brinkman P, van der Schee MP, Fens N, Dijkers E, Bootsma SK, de Jongh FH, Sterk PJ, "Integration of electronic nose technology with spirometry: validation of a new approach for exhaled breath analysis." J Breath Res 9(4):046001. PMID 26469298, DOI 10.1088/1752-7155/9/4/046001 [A]. This is the founding SpiroNose paper. Hardware: "five identical metal oxide semiconductor sensor arrays (three arrays monitoring exhaled breath and two reference arrays monitoring ambient air) at the rear end of a pneumotachograph". Raw sensor deflection correlated with expiratory flow (R² = 0.84, n = 60 healthy subjects). Sensor readings did not correlate with exhaled volume, humidity or temperature. After environment correction based on **alveolar gradients**, sensor arrays were highly reproducible (Cohen's kappa 0.81-0.94). In a two-centre case-control study (37 asthma, 31 COPD, 31 lung cancer, 45 controls), PCA plus discriminant analysis in Matlab2014/SPSS20 gave CVV of 78-88%. The stated aim was bringing eNose to the "point-of-care" by integrating it with routine spirometry. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26469298/
- 2018. de Vries R et al. (Sterk PJ senior), "Clinical and inflammatory phenotyping by breathomics in chronic airway diseases irrespective of the diagnostic label." Eur Respir J 51(1):1701817. PMID 29326334, DOI 10.1183/13993003.01817-2017 [A]. Multicentre study of 435 asthma/COPD patients (training 321, validation 114). PCA followed by unsupervised clustering gave 5 clusters defined by inflammatory traits rather than diagnosis. Linear regression from eNose predicted blood eosinophils (R² = 0.581) and neutrophils (R² = 0.409), with replication in the validation set. The abstract does not name the device. That it was the SpiroNose is inferred from the authors and timing (UNVERIFIED from text). — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29326334/
- 2019. de Vries R et al. (Sterk PJ, van den Heuvel MM senior), "Prediction of response to anti-PD-1 therapy in patients with non-small-cell lung cancer by electronic nose analysis of exhaled breath." Ann Oncol 30(10):1660-1666. PMID 31529107, DOI 10.1093/annonc/mdz279 [A]. 143 NSCLC patients (training 92, validation 51), using an MOS eNose at the rear of a pneumotachograph. Analysis was t-tests followed by LDA and ROC. Baseline breath vs 3-month RECIST response: AUC 0.89 in training and 0.85 in validation. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31529107/
- 2020. Abdel-Aziz MI et al., "eNose breath prints as a surrogate biomarker for classifying patients with asthma by atopy." J Allergy Clin Immunol. PMID 32531371, DOI 10.1016/j.jaci.2020.05.038. Title from PubMed. Details come from a 2026 secondary review table (PMC13313609 [FT]): n = 655 across BreathCloud, U-BIOPRED and PACMAN2, using **both Cyranose 320 and SpiroNose**, with PLS-DA and Bayesian network analysis, and AUROC ≥ 0.85 across cohorts. Primary abstract not read, so details are UNVERIFIED from primary source. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32531371/ ; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13313609
- 2020 (published 2021). van Bragt JJMH et al., "Identification of recent exacerbations in COPD patients by electronic nose." ERJ Open Res. PMID 33447611, DOI 10.1183/23120541.00307-2020, PMC7792783 [FT]. Describes "the SpiroNose (Amsterdam UMC, Amsterdam, the Netherlands), an eNose that uses seven cross-reactive metal oxide semiconducting sensors". The measurement protocol: mouth rinse, 5 tidal breaths, full inspiration, 5 s breath-hold, slow maximal expiration, in duplicate 2 min apart, with data captured in real time. The manufacturer is credited here as Amsterdam UMC, not Breathomix. — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7792783

### Inferences
- The methodological arc runs from small n = 30-40 case-control studies with in-sample cross-validation (2007-09), to external validation (2011), to self-critique of the field's validation practice (2015 Leopold), to multi-site standardisation (U-BIOPRED 2018; ERS 2017 standard), to prospective real-world cohorts and prospective external validation (2023-2026).
- The 2025 Ann Oncol result is informative for builders. A model validated in its target population (COPD, AUC 0.92) fell to AUC 0.80 in a broader population and needed retraining. Breathprint models appear population-specific.
- Increasingly, SpiroNose studies are led by other centres (Erasmus MC ILD, Toronto CF, NKI lung cancer, LUMC COVID). Sterk's own authorship tails off after about 2023 in these searches.

### Gaps
- Full texts of the 2007 Dragonieri, 2015 de Vries J Breath Res and 2019 Brinkman JACI papers were not read: they are not open access in Europe PMC.
- The SpiroNose is used in the 2018 ERJ phenotyping paper, but this is not confirmed from the abstract.
- No peer-reviewed version of the de Vries COVID-19 public-health study was found in these searches. Only the medRxiv preprint record was found, and it was not read.
- The individual device for each 2010-2016 Cyranose-era application paper was not verified.

## Q2. Devices over time, and why they changed

### Takeaway
The lineage is: Cyranose 320 (commercial, 32-sensor carbon black-polymer, Tedlar-bag sampling, 2007 onward), then a four-device composite of Cyranose 320, Tor Vergata QCM, Common Invent MOS and Owlstone Lonestar FAIMS, run centrally on sorbent tubes in U-BIOPRED, then the in-house SpiroNose (MOS arrays with ambient reference sensors mounted behind a spirometer, 2015 onward), which became cloud-connected and is now sold by Breathomix. The papers give these reasons for change: point-of-care integration with spirometry, ambient-air correction, and the cross-device incompatibility and pooling problems the group itself raised. No paper found states a single explicit rationale for leaving the Cyranose.

### Cited Findings
- Cyranose 320 with Tedlar-bag sampling and VOC-filtered inspiration was used in 2007, 2009 and 2010. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17658592/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18834643/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22163399/
- The Cyranose 320 is a carbon black-polymer sensor array (secondary review). The "32 polymer sensors" figure was NOT confirmed in any record opened here: UNVERIFIED in these notes. — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7763696
- The group's 2013 review lists "incompatibility between eNoses" as a key methodological issue and proposes a pooled "Breath Cloud" database. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23786277/
- In U-BIOPRED and the mesothelioma study, four eNoses of different sensor technologies were used on thermally desorbed Tenax tubes, and their sensor deflections were merged. — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC5710949 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30529449/
- The 2013 group paper studied transport and storage of breath samples on sorbent tubes for eNose analysis (title only). — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23257711/
- The SpiroNose was introduced to "bring this technology to 'point-of-care'" by integrating it with routine spirometry. The 2015 description is five identical MOS arrays (3 exhaled, 2 ambient reference) at the rear end of a pneumotachograph. Its signal depends on expiratory flow. Environmental correction is based on alveolar gradients. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26469298/
- Later descriptions say seven cross-reactive MOS sensors (S1-S7), with a second identical set sampling ambient air (2020 ERJ Open Res; 2023 Chest). These are the same device family described at a different level, an array of 7 sensors rather than "5 arrays". How the 2015 and later descriptions map onto each other was not resolved from the texts read. — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7792783 ; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10635840
- How the device is credited changed over time: "SpiroNose (Amsterdam UMC, Amsterdam)" in 2020/21, "SpiroNose; Breathomix" in 2023, and "SpiroNose® (Breathomix, Leiden)" in 2026. The 2023 and 2025 papers describe it as cloud-connected, with real-time upload to an online analysis platform. — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7792783 ; https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10635840 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41881763/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40174676/
- The Breathomix "List of Publications" (company document v33, 2 Feb 2026, local file /home/chris/sw-enose/reports/20260202-V33-Breathomix-List-of-Publications.pdf) is a lead only. Its earliest entry is de Vries 2015 J Breath Res, followed by de Vries 2018 ERJ and then the 2019 Ann Oncol paper. No Cyranose-era papers appear in it. This is consistent with SpiroNose being used in the 2018 ERJ phenotyping study, though the abstract does not name the device (still UNVERIFIED from primary text). The list also includes the de Vries 2021 SARS-CoV-2 medRxiv preprint with authors de Vries R, Vigeveno RM, ... Sterk PJ, in 't Veen JCCM, Groeneveld GH, and has no peer-reviewed citation for it.
- Cyranose 320 and SpiroNose data were used together across cohorts as late as 2020 (atopy paper; secondary source). — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13313609

### Inferences
- From the sources, the engineering motives for the SpiroNose appear to be: (a) removing the bag, sorbent tube and transport step, (b) standardising the breath manoeuvre through the spirometer, (c) correcting for ambient air with reference sensors, and (d) a device the group controls and can deploy at many sites, feeding a central database (BreathCloud). These are inferred from the stated aims and design. No quoted "we abandoned the Cyranose because..." statement was found.
- For an open-source builder, the reference-sensor ambient correction and the flow dependence (R² = 0.84 with expiratory flow) are the most transferable design points.

### Gaps
- The exact MOS sensor part numbers and manufacturer in the SpiroNose were not found in the texts read. The 2015 J Breath Res full text would be the place to check.
- No source was found that explicitly explains why the group left the Cyranose 320.

## Q3. Biologically inspired / neuromorphic processing, or conventional?

### Takeaway
Every Sterk-group paper examined used conventional statistics and pattern recognition. None used neuromorphic, spiking or explicitly biologically inspired processing. The methods were PCA, then LDA or canonical discriminant analysis, ROC, PLS-DA, t-tests, linear regression, unsupervised clustering (Ward, K-means, PAM, topological data analysis), mixed models, PERMANOVA and Bayesian networks.

### Cited Findings
- 2007: LDA on principal-component reduction, with cross-validation. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17658592/
- 2009: Cyranose "onboard statistical software". — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18834643/
- 2011: PCA plus canonical discriminant analysis and ROC. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21732998/
- 2015 SpiroNose: signal processing, alveolar-gradient environment correction, PCA then discriminant analysis (Matlab/SPSS). — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26469298/
- 2015 Leopold: compared published dimension-reduction and classification combinations and found none consistently better between internal and external validation. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26669708/
- 2018 ERJ: PCA, unsupervised clustering, linear regression. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29326334/
- 2019 JACI U-BIOPRED: Ward clustering with similarity-profile analysis, K-means, PAM, topological data analysis. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30529449/
- 2019 Ann Oncol and 2021 Lung Cancer: t-tests, LDA, ROC. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31529107/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34399166/
- 2023 Chest: PCA, LDA, ROC, plus ambient-air correction. — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10635840
- Collaborator-led SpiroNose papers: PLS-DA (ILD 2021, 2026); linear mixed models and PERMANOVA (CF 2026). — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32732331/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41738130/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41881763/
- A Europe PMC query for author "Sterk PJ" with neuromorphic, spiking, bio-inspired, biologically inspired, artificial neural network or neural network returned 1 hit. It was a 2005 Thorax asthma paper unrelated to eNose processing (PMID 16061704). — https://europepmc.org/search?query=AUTH%3A%22Sterk%20PJ%22%20AND%20neural%20network
- The furthest step toward machine learning in this lineage found here is collaborator-led, not Sterk-led. van der Sar 2023 (Erasmus MC/TU Delft) benchmarked classifiers on SpiroNose-type data, and feature selection plus random forest performed best. This is conventional ML, not neuromorphic. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37595574/
- 2021 rhinovirus study: per-sensor deviation from an individual baseline, tested with Mann-Whitney and ROC. This is simple statistics, though it is a personalised-baseline design. — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33704785/
- Artificial neural networks do appear in the wider eNose literature, for example Aeonose CF studies by other groups, as listed in a 2026 review table. These are not Sterk-group studies. — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13313609

### Inferences
- The group's own emphasis is on validation design (external sets, prospective cohorts, multi-site batch effects) and on sensor and ambient correction. It is not on classifier sophistication. The 2015 Leopold result suggests classifier choice mattered less than validation design.

### Gaps
- Breathomix's proprietary "advanced signal processing" and online platform algorithms are not described in detail in any abstract read. Whether they contain ML beyond PCA/LDA is unknown.
- "Not found in these searches" applies: an exhaustive full-text search of all 74 papers was not done.

## Q4. Relationship with Breathomix

### Takeaway
Breathomix BV is described in the group's papers as a "start-up company" (Leiden). Rianne de Vries, first author of the SpiroNose papers, is COO with a "considerable" or "substantial interest". Peter Sterk is a scientific adviser with a small interest: a "4% interest" in a 2019/2020 ERJ disclosure, later described as "formally inconsiderable" or "officially nonsubstantial". Other authors (Farzan N, Dagelet YWF, Slingers G) received personal fees or are affiliated with Breathomix. The SpiroNose was credited to Amsterdam UMC as late as 2020/21. That supports, but does not prove, that Breathomix commercialises an Amsterdam UMC-developed device. The terms "spin-off" and "founders" were NOT confirmed in any source opened.

### Cited Findings
- Brinkman 2020 ERJ COI: "P.J. Sterk ... reports a 4% interest and is a scientific advisor for Breathomix BV, outside the submitted work." — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31515400/

Sterk reports being scientific advisor to and having a formally inconsiderable interest in the start-up company Breathomix BV." In the same paper de Vries is affiliated with "Breathomix BV, Leiden" and the device is credited to "SpiroNose (Amsterdam UMC, Amsterdam, the Netherlands)". — https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7792783
- de Vries 2023 Chest disclosures [FT]: "R. d. V. receives personal fees and has a substantial interest in the start-up company Breathomix BV. N. F. and Y. W. F. D. receive personal fees from the start-up company Breathomix BV. P. J. S. is scientific adviser and has an officially nonsubstantial interest in the start-up company Breathomix BV." ### Inferences
- The device moved from an academic credit to a company credit between about 2020 and 2023, and the lead device developer is the company's COO. Together these strongly suggest Breathomix commercialises the AMC/Amsterdam UMC SpiroNose. This is an inference, not a confirmed "spin-off" statement.
- Many SpiroNose validation studies have a Breathomix-interested author (de Vries) on the author list. This is relevant when weighing independence. The ILD studies (Moor 2021, Formsma 2026) and the LUMC COVID study do not list de Vries in the abstract author lists seen.

### Gaps
